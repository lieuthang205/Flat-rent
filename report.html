What will this flat rent for — with and without a car space?

Weekly rent estimates and parking premiums across all 65,777 flats in the City of Sydney (as at 31 July 2026)

Across all 65,777 City of Sydney flats, a dedicated car space adds a median of $36.50 a week ($1,898 a year) in estimated weekly rent — rising from $20.50/week for studio apartments to $78.00/week for 3-bedroom apartments. By combining a bedroom-adjusted Holt-Winters time-series index with a monotonic HistGradientBoostingRegressor trained under absolute-error loss and leakage-safe building/flat rent history, our model achieved a held-out median absolute error of $42.50/week (with 81.0% of predictions within $100/week and R² = 0.906).

What we found along the way

+$36.50 / wk

Median parking premium across all 65,777 City of Sydney flats (interquartile range $24.00 to $55.00/wk), equivalent to ~$1,900 per year.

$34 vs $53

Flats without a car space had a $34.00/wk median error (87.7% within $100, R² = 0.921), while flats with a car space had a $53.00/wk median error (75.7% within $100).

4.2× lower error

Including building-level and unit-level prior rent history cut the median error from $180.00/wk (suburb × bedroom median benchmark) down to $42.50/wk.

What our separate held-out check showed

To test the model strictly out-of-time without data leakage, we fitted the monthly index, historical building/flat features, and gradient boosting model using only earlier adverts, and evaluated predictions against later adverts dated up to 31 July 2026. Those held-out adverts were never used to build or tune the method.

What data supported the result?

We used the three official datasets supplied for the City of Sydney task, strictly enforcing the 31 July 2026 as-at rule:

  • rents-input-flats-city-of-sydney.csv (65,777 flats): Flat-level coordinates, suburb, meshblock (mb), bedrooms, bathrooms, car spaces, floor level, building size, and listing history metadata.
  • rents-past-leases-city-of-sydney.csv (112,457 adverts): Filtered to adverts dated ≤ 31 July 2026 with weekly rents between $150 and $5,000/wk, de-duplicated and linked to 105,594 input flat records.
  • blocks-homes-and-people-2021-2026.csv (2,281 blocks): Joined by meshblock mb to capture 2021–2026 residential density and new-home supply growth.

How did we estimate rent?

Our pipeline separates market-wide rent inflation from property-specific hedonic and historical attributes:

  1. Holt-Winters Monthly Rent Index: We compute a monthly bedroom-adjusted median log rent index and tune additive Holt-Winters smoothing weights (α, β, γ) via L-BFGS-B to de-trend historical rents and project the index to August–September 2026.
  2. Leakage-Safe Historical Anchors: For each advert (and target flat), we compute strictly prior de-trended log rents at three levels: the exact flat (own_prev_lp), same building × bedroom count (bld_bed_prev_lp), and same building overall (bld_prev_lp).
  3. Monotonic Gradient Boosting (L1 Loss): We train a HistGradientBoostingRegressor with loss="absolute_error" (directly optimising median dollar error) and monotonic constraints on bedrooms, bathrooms, and car spaces, then score each flat twice (car_space = 1 and 0) and round to $0.50.

How did we check it?

We performed a strict temporal split before building the final submission:

  • Primary 2-Month Check (matching the 2-month Aug–Sep scoring horizon): Trained on 102,941 adverts dated before 1 June 2026; tested on 2,653 later adverts from 1 June to 31 July 2026 → $42.50/wk median error, 80.96% within $100, R² = 0.906.
  • Extended 3-Month Check: Trained on 101,728 adverts dated before 1 May 2026; tested on 3,866 later adverts from 1 May to 31 July 2026 → $45.50/wk median error, 79.98% within $100, R² = 0.898.
  • In both checks, the monthly index, historical features, and boosting trees were fitted exclusively on the earlier build split.

What remains uncertain?

While 81.0% of held-out predictions land within $100/week, 133 of 2,653 June–July adverts (5.0%) missed by more than $200/week:

  • Unobserved interior renovations & harbour views: High-floor luxury or newly renovated apartments in Sydney CBD, Barangaroo, and Darlinghurst can command $300–$800/week premiums over unrenovated units on the same floor.
  • First-time-listed buildings: Flats in buildings with zero prior rental history rely on meshblock, suburb, and physical attributes rather than building-specific rent history.
